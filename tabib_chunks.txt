1 Project Overview
Vero is an integrated productivity ecosystem designed to eliminate the friction between
communication and documentation. Conceived as a ”fusion” of high-performance video
conferencing and comprehensive workspace management, Vero provides a single application
environment that replaces the need for separate, disconnected tools.
The platform is built around a Shared Project Context that tightly couples three core
modules:
----------------------------------------

--- Chunk 7 ---
Page: 3
Source: tabib.pdf
Sub-chunk: 2/4

modules:
• Meet Module: A P2P audio and video conferencing suite featuring screen sharing
and automated meeting note initialization.
• Notes Module: A collaborative rich text editor that utilizes slash commands and
simultaneous note-taking capabilities.
• Board Module: A workflow planning tool featuring Kanban boards where tasks are
dynamically linked to meeting notes and real-time checklists. Subsequently, people can
be assigned tasks in a team and maintained by a manager.

2 Motivation
The primary motivation behind Vero is to address the fragmented nature of modern remote
team work and academic collaboration. Currently, teams are forced to juggle at least two
separate applications for video conferencing, documentation, and task assignment. This
fragmentation leads to several critical pain points:
1. Context Switching: Valuable time and focus are lost when users must manually
transfer information from a meeting (Zoom/Teams) to a document (Notion/Docs) and

2. Information Silos: Action items discussed during live calls often fail to be docu-
mented, leading to ”meeting amnesia” where decisions are lost or forgotten.
3. Lack of Synchronization: Changesinaproject’stasklist(Board)arerarelyreflected
automaticallyintherelateddocumentation,causingadisconnectbetweenplanningand
execution.

3 The Case for Unified Workspaces: Past Research
Findings
While modern collaborative tools like Zoom and Notion are powerful, the necessity of switch-
ing between them creates a significant ”Tax on Productivity.” Vero is designed specifically
to mitigate these scientifically documented cognitive costs.
• Rubinstein, Meyer & Evans (2001) – The Switch Cost
Rubinstein, Meyer, and Evans at the University of Michigan ran four controlled exper-

types of tasks, subjects lost time when switching, and time costs increased with com-

for the old task and turning on rules for the new one), both consuming measurable time
and cognitive resources.
